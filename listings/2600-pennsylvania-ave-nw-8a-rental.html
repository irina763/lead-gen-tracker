The building

An Art Deco mid-rise on Pennsylvania Avenue.

Built in 2001 in an Art Deco idiom, the building sits at the West End edge of Foggy Bottom — eight floors, elevator service, intercom entry, and an attached garage. #8A is a top-floor unit, so there is no one overhead.

Day to day it reads as a quiet, small-building address rather than a high-rise: you take one elevator, you park under your own roof, and the front door opens onto one of the best-connected corners in the city.

    The location

    West End — between Georgetown and the river.

    From above: the building, the avenue, and the waterfront and parkland that frame it. Tap any image to enlarge.

    2600 Pennsylvania Avenue NW sits where West End meets Georgetown — the avenue running east toward the White House, Rock Creek and the Potomac a short walk west. It is one of the few DC addresses where a car is genuinely optional and a garage space still comes with the lease.

    Georgetown's M Street shops and restaurants, the Georgetown Waterfront, the Kennedy Center, Trader Joe's and Whole Foods, GW Hospital and the university campus are all within an easy walk — and Foggy Bottom–GWU Metro puts three lines within reach.
